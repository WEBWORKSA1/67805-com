Choosing an AI assistant for your team

How to decide between general assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot, using criteria that will still matter after the next update.

By the 67805.com editorial teamUpdated October 27, 20268 min read

The base layer of every AI stack is a general assistant. The leading options update constantly, so any feature-by-feature comparison is out of date within weeks. A better approach is to choose using criteria that stay stable, then run a short trial on your own work.

The main options

  • ChatGPT (OpenAI): a broad, popular assistant with a large ecosystem of features and integrations.
  • Claude (Anthropic): an assistant with a reputation for long documents, careful writing and coding help.
  • Gemini (Google): an assistant closely tied to Gmail, Docs, Drive and the rest of Google Workspace.
  • Microsoft Copilot: an assistant built into Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams for Microsoft 365 users.

All four are capable. The right one depends more on how your business works than on benchmark scores.

Five criteria that still matter next year

1. Where your work already lives

If your team lives in Google Workspace, Gemini’s integration is a real advantage. If you run on Microsoft 365, Copilot sits inside the apps you use all day. If your tools are mixed, a standalone assistant that connects to many services may suit you better.

2. The work you do most

Test on your actual tasks: long contracts, client emails, data in spreadsheets, code, images. Quality differences show up quickly on real work and rarely show in marketing pages.

3. Data terms for business

Check the business or team plans for how your inputs are handled, admin controls, and where data is stored. Consumer plans and business plans often have different terms.

4. Admin and sharing

For teams, look at shared workspaces or projects, user management, single sign-on and the ability to share prompts and documents.

5. Total cost

Multiply the per-seat price by the number of people who will genuinely use it. Not everyone needs a paid seat on day one.

Run a fair two-week trial

  1. Pick five real tasks your team does every week.
  2. Write one prompt per task and run it in two assistants.
  3. Have the people who do that work rate the outputs blind, without knowing which tool produced which.
  4. Note time saved and how much editing each output needed.
  5. Choose the winner for the base layer. You can still use others for specific jobs.

Should you use more than one?

Many teams standardize on one assistant for everyday work and allow a second for specialist tasks. That keeps training and billing simple while leaving room for the best tool per job.
